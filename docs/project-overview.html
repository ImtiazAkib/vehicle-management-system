Fleet maintenance platform · VUSMS

Vehicle Utilities Supply & Maintenance System

Owners profile every vehicle and put every service on a schedule; a nightly worker warns them before maintenance falls due; and they book admin-verified service centers and order parts from verified suppliers — with role-scoped dashboards for owners, service centers, suppliers, administrators and a single seeded super-admin.

What it centralizes

Owners track maintenance by memory and keep records in a glovebox. Three failures follow — missed service dates, lost history, unreachable garages — and each one becomes a single source of truth.

Service dates kept in your head A schedule that computes what is due

Each vehicle carries interval rules by months or mileage; a worker advances UPCOMING → DUE → OVERDUE and files a reminder before the date passes, not after.

Receipts in the glovebox A maintenance history that persists

Every service writes a MaintenanceRecord with mileage and cost in integer minor units — the vehicle's log outlives the paper and the owner.

Calling around for a garage Verified centers and suppliers, one tap away

Owners book only admin-verified service centers and order only from verified suppliers; an unverified account is visible but not transactable.

● full · ○ oversight (verify and govern, not transact) · — no access. Exactly one super-admin exists — a partial unique index on role permits a single seeded row, immutable even to itself, and the only account that can govern admins. Admin governs accounts and reads platform-wide reports but deliberately never books, sells, ships or invoices on anyone's behalf; governance and business are separate hands. Role is immutable after creation, and nobody edits their own role. A hidden button is not a closed door — the rule lives in the service.

How a request is served

The layered path every write takes — from the same-origin proxy to the DTO that comes back. Its point is the return arrow: a rejected action comes back as data the form can render field by field, not as an exception someone has to catch.

How a reminder fires

The feature the platform exists for. A schedule that falls due is worth nothing until someone is told — so a separate worker turns a crossed date into a notification, exactly once.

The data model

Ten core collections. Two of them — User and Vehicle — are referenced by everything else, and the maintenance this platform automates lives on the one entity that carries a VIN, a mileage and a due date.

What the database guarantees, not the code

A check in a service is a rule the next request can interleave; an index is the one it cannot. The guarantees that matter here are writes.

Guarantee Enforced by
Money never drifts Every price, cost, fee and total is an integer count of minor units — never a float. Floating-point money is a bug with a delay on it.
A booking and an order move only along legal transitions accept / decline / start / complete / cancel are action methods behind a legal-transition guard — there is no raw status write to smuggle a booking from PENDING to COMPLETED .
Exactly one super-admin exists A partial unique index over { role: 'super-admin' } permits a single row — seeded once, never created through a screen, immutable even to itself.
The last unit of stock sells once OrderService decrements stock atomically against the ceiling; the oversell matches nothing and the order is refused — the same shape that mints tickets once in the sibling platform.
A VIN, a registration and a SKU are unique Unique indexes reject the duplicate at write time — not after a read-then-write race the next request can interleave.
